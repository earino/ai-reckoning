# About this investigation

## Authorship

*The AI Reckoning* was researched, analysed, coded and written by **Claude**, an AI system made by Anthropic, working autonomously from a written brief supplied by Eduardo Arino de la Rubia, who commissioned the project. No human reported, edited or fact-checked individual claims before publication.

That matters for how you should read it:

- Treat it as a transparent, reproducible analysis, not as reporting by a newsroom with human editors.
- Every number can be traced to a data file, and every data file to code and a source.
- Where the AI system could not read a primary document, the text says so.

## Conflict of interest

Anthropic, the maker of the AI system that produced this work, is a subject of it: its stock-market listing is part of the event, and it is one of the two most connected entities in the relationship graph. The AI system was not instructed by Anthropic about this project. We applied the same rules to Anthropic as to every other company. Readers should weigh the conflict.

No one paid for or sponsored this work. It is not investment advice. The AI system holds no investments. The commissioner's personal holdings were not reviewed.

## How automation was used

- **Source research.** AI research agents searched the web and compiled the chronology and the relationship dataset, with a source link per item.
- **Extraction.** Financial figures come from structured SEC data by code, not from language-model reading. Relationship amounts were extracted by AI agents from releases and articles and carry confidence grades.
- **Analysis.** Hypotheses were written down and committed before return data were downloaded.
- **Writing and graphics.** Written by the AI system from the computed results. Numbers in the text are inserted from the data files when the page loads.
- **Checks.** Automated tests for data, statistics, application logic, accessibility and the deployed site.

## Method in one paragraph

We chose 215 listed companies by rule, measured each one's sensitivity to the market before the selloff, and compared what happened with what that sensitivity predicted. We read company accounts to measure spending and cash generation, and built a sourced map of 196 investment, lending and purchase relationships. See [Methodology](#/methodology) and [Limitations](#/limitations).
